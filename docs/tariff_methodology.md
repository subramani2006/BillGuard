# Tamil Nadu Electricity Tariff Methodology & Regulatory Framework

## 1. Regulatory Context
Domestic electricity consumption in Tamil Nadu is governed by tariff orders issued by the **Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission (TNERC)** and implemented by the state power utility, **Tamil Nadu Generation and Distribution Corporation Ltd. (TANGEDCO)**.

Domestic residences fall under **Low Tension Tariff I-A (LT Tariff 1-A)**.

---

## 2. Core Billing Principles

### 2.1 Bi-Monthly Billing Cycle
Unlike many northern Indian states that practice monthly billing, Tamil Nadu predominantly employs a **bi-monthly billing cycle** (standard: 60 days). Meters are inspected and bills assessed every two months by utility meter assessors.

### 2.2 Government of Tamil Nadu (GoTN) Lifeline Subsidy
Under policy directives from the Government of Tamil Nadu, the **first 100 units consumed per bi-monthly cycle are 100% subsidized (free of charge)** for all domestic consumers.
- If a consumer uses 85 units, the net energy charge is **₹0.00**.
- If a consumer uses 250 units, the first 100 units remain **₹0.00**, while the remaining 150 units are assessed under subsequent tiered slabs.

## 3. Delayed Billing Cycle & The Slab Jump Grievance
A major recurring consumer grievance in Tamil Nadu arises when meter reading is delayed beyond the standard 60 days (e.g., to 75 or 85 days).
Because electricity tariffs are progressive, assessing 75 days of usage against a 60-day slab limit artificially forces ordinary daily consumption into higher, punitive slabs:

### Fair Proration Formula
$$\text{Prorated Limit} = \text{Base Slab Limit} \times \left(\frac{\text{Actual Billing Days}}{60}\right)$$

BillGuard explicitly detects un-prorated extended cycles, computes the monetary penalty incurred by the consumer, and includes this evidence in the grievance draft.

## 4. Statutory Consumer Protection Clauses
- **Clause 11 of the Tamil Nadu Electricity Distribution Code**: Mandates immediate rectification of incorrect, erroneous, or clerical billing assessments upon consumer representation.
- **Rule 57 of the Central Electricity Authority (CEA) Meter Regulations**: Grants consumers the legal right to demand meter testing using an official accredited test meter if an abnormal surge or recording fault is suspected.
